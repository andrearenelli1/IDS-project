\begin{abstract}
This report presents a fully distributed multi-drone system for ARTVA
(Apparecchio di Ricerca Travolti in Valanga) source localization in
search and rescue scenarios.  A fleet of $N$ autonomous drones
cooperatively estimates the position of a buried victim by combining:
(i) Model Predictive Control (MPC) for smooth waypoint-following in
real terrain; (ii) decentralized cooperative localization via the
IMDCL algorithm based on inter-agent relative measurements;
(iii) distributed gradient descent (DCGD) for source position
estimation; and (iv) consensus-based partner selection for
triangulation.  The system operates without a central coordinator;
each drone maintains only local state information and communicates
within a limited radio range $R_c = 80\,$m.  Extensive parameter
sweeps over five distinct terrain patches from the real TINItaly DEM,
randomized victim positions, randomized burial depths, and varying
communication radii confirm sub-meter localization accuracy under
realistic noise conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Avalanche rescue operations require rapid and precise localization of
buried victims.  ARTVA transceivers emit a magnetic dipole signal that
can be detected by search parties or, increasingly, by autonomous
aerial vehicles.  The challenge lies in coordinating multiple
drones---each with partial, noisy information---to converge on the
source location without centralized control.

This project addresses four tightly coupled sub-problems:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Path-following}: each drone must track a sequence of
        waypoints defined by a high-level mission planner, subject to
        actuator constraints, using only its locally estimated state.
  \item \textbf{Self-localization}: each drone must accurately estimate
        its own 6-DOF state (position and velocity) without absolute
        positioning, relying on inter-drone relative measurements and
        altimeter data.
  \item \textbf{Source estimation}: the fleet must cooperatively
        minimize a nonlinear least-squares cost over ARTVA signal
        measurements.
  \item \textbf{Coordination}: once a drone detects a strong enough
        signal, it must recruit the nearest reachable partners for
        triangulation, purely through local communication.
\end{enumerate}

Related work on distributed ARTVA search~\cite{artva2020} and
cooperative Kalman filtering~\cite{kia2016cooperative} motivates our
algorithmic choices.  Average consensus~\cite{olfati2004consensus} and
diffusion-based optimization~\cite{chen2012diffusion} provide the
theoretical backbone of the source estimation approach.  The
receding-horizon MPC strategy for drone trajectory tracking follows the
framework of~\cite{rawlings2017mpc}.

\section{Adopted Models}
\label{sec:models}

\subsection{Communication System}

At each simulation step $t$, drones communicate over an undirected
graph $\mathcal{G}(t) = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}(t))$ where
$\mathcal{V} = \{1,\ldots,N\}$ and an edge $(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$
exists iff $\|\mathbf{p}_i(t) - \mathbf{p}_j(t)\| \leq R_c$, with
$R_c = 80\,$m.  Communication is symmetric and range-limited; no global
topology information is available to any single agent.

\subsection{Drone Dynamics}

Each drone is modelled as a 3-D point mass controlled in acceleration.
The state and input vectors are:
\[
  x_i = \begin{bmatrix} p_x & p_y & p_z & v_x & v_y & v_z \end{bmatrix}^\top,
  \quad
  u_i = \begin{bmatrix} a_x & a_y & a_z \end{bmatrix}^\top.
\]
Using a Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) discretization with step $dt$:
\begin{equation}
  x_i(k+1) = F\,x_i(k) + G\,u_i(k) + G\,\eta_i(k),
  \label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q)$ is process noise with isotropic
acceleration variance $\sigma_{acc}^2 = (0.05\,\text{m/s}^2)^2$, and
\[
  F = \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & dt\,I_3 \\ 0_3 & I_3 \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  G = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{1}{2}dt^2\,I_3 \\ dt\,I_3 \end{bmatrix}.
\]

\subsection{ARTVA Signal Model}

The buried victim is modelled as a vertical magnetic dipole with moment
$m$ at position $\bm{\theta}^* \in \mathbb{R}^3$.  The noiseless
signal received at position $\mathbf{p}$ is:
\begin{equation}
  S(\mathbf{p}, \bm{\theta}) =
  m\,\frac{\sqrt{1 + 3\cos^2\!\vartheta}}{r^3}, \quad
  r = \|\mathbf{p} - \bm{\theta}\|,
  \label{eq:artva}
\end{equation}
with $\cos\vartheta = (\mathbf{p} - \bm{\theta})_z / r$.
Noisy measurements are
$\tilde{S}_i(t) = S(\mathbf{p}_i(t), \bm{\theta}^*) + \varepsilon_i(t)$,
where $\varepsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and
$\sigma = 10^{-6}$.

\section{Proposed Solution}
\label{sec:solution}

\subsection{MPC Path-Following Controller}

Each drone tracks a sequence of waypoints generated by the mission
planner using a receding-horizon MPC formulation.  At every time step
the MPC uses the IMDCL state estimate $\hat{x}_i^+$ as the initial
condition and solves:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{u_0,\ldots,u_{N-1}} J
  = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \!\bigl[w_p \|p_k - p_{\mathrm{ref}}\|^2
    + w_a \|u_k\|^2\bigr]
    + w_{fp} \|p_N - p_{\mathrm{ref}}\|^2
    + w_{fv} \|v_N\|^2
  \label{eq:mpc_cost}
\end{equation}
subject to the linear dynamics~\eqref{eq:dynamics} and the box
constraints:
\begin{equation}
  \|v_j(k)\|_\infty \leq v_{\max}, \quad
  \|u_j(k)\|_\infty \leq a_{\max},
  \quad j \in \{x,y,z\}.
  \label{eq:mpc_constr}
\end{equation}
The problem is solved with the IPOPT interior-point
solver~\cite{rawlings2017mpc} via the CasADi symbolic
framework.

\paragraph{Warm-starting.}
At first activation the solver runs up to $N_{\mathrm{init}} = 1000$
IPOPT iterations to reach a high-quality solution.  Subsequent MPC
steps reuse the shifted optimal trajectory as initial guess and limit
iterations to $N_{\mathrm{mpc}} = 3$, keeping the solve time below
$5\,$ms on average.

\paragraph{Waypoint sequencing.}
When the estimated distance to the current waypoint drops below the
arrival threshold $\delta = 0.3\,$m, the mission planner advances to
the next waypoint and updates $p_{\mathrm{ref}}$ without resetting the
warm-start, so the solver transitions smoothly between consecutive
targets.

\subsection{IMDCL: Decentralized Cooperative Localization}

The \emph{Interim Master Decentralized Cooperative Localization}
(IMDCL)~\cite{kia2016cooperative} allows each drone to track its state
with a local EKF while accounting for cross-covariance terms arising
from shared information, without maintaining a global covariance
matrix.  This is essential in a GPS-denied environment where only
relative inter-drone measurements and onboard altimeter data are
available.

Each agent $i$ maintains $(\hat{x}_i, P_i, \Phi_i, \{\Pi_{jl}^i\})$.
The \textbf{propagation} step follows~\eqref{eq:dynamics}:
\begin{align}
  \hat{x}_i^-(k+1) &= F\,\hat{x}_i^+(k) + G\,u_i(k), \nonumber\\
  P_i^-(k+1) &= F\,P_i^+(k)\,F^\top + G\,Q\,G^\top, \nonumber\\
  \Phi_i(k+1) &= F\,\Phi_i(k).
\end{align}

Two measurement sources update the filter at each step:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{LiDAR altimeter}: absolute altitude above terrain
        with noise $\sigma_{\mathrm{LiDAR}} = 0.05\,$m.
  \item \textbf{Inter-drone relative ranging}: 3-D relative position
        between each pair of drones within $R_c$, with noise
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0.3\,$m.
\end{itemize}

When agent $a$ (interim master) observes agent $b$ via relative
position measurement $z_{ab} = p_b - p_a + \nu_{ab}$, the innovation
is $r_a = z_{ab} - (\hat{p}_b^- - \hat{p}_a^-)$, and the innovation
covariance accounts for cross-correlations:
\begin{equation}
  S_{ab} = R_{ab}
    + \tilde{H}_a P_a^- \tilde{H}_a^\top
    + \tilde{H}_b P_b^- \tilde{H}_b^\top
    - \tilde{H}_a \Phi_a \Pi_{ab}^a \Phi_b^\top \tilde{H}_b^\top
    - (\cdot)^\top,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{H}_a = [-I_3\ 0_3]$ and $\tilde{H}_b = [I_3\ 0_3]$.
Each agent $i$ then updates:
\begin{align}
  \hat{x}_i^+ &= \hat{x}_i^- + \Phi_i\,\Gamma_i\,\tilde{r}_a, \\
  P_i^+ &= P_i^- - \Phi_i\,\Gamma_i\,\Gamma_i^\top\,\Phi_i^\top,
\end{align}
with $\tilde{r}_a = S_{ab}^{-1/2}r_a$ and gain terms $\Gamma_i$
computed from local information (see~\cite{kia2016cooperative}).
After each update, $\Phi_i \leftarrow I$.

\subsection{DCGD: Distributed Source Estimation}

TRACK drones cooperatively minimize the global cost:
\begin{equation}
  J(\bm{\theta}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}}
    \sum_{(\mathbf{p},\tilde{s}) \in \mathcal{B}_i}
    \bigl(\tilde{s} - S(\mathbf{p}, \bm{\theta})\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_i$ is a sliding batch of
$|\mathcal{B}_i| = 5$ measurements.
Each DCGD iteration consists of two steps.

\paragraph{Adapt.}
Each drone performs a normalized gradient descent step:
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\theta}_i^{(k+\frac{1}{2})} = \bm{\theta}_i^{(k)}
  - \alpha\,\frac{\bar{g}_i(\bm{\theta}_i^{(k)})}
                 {\|\bar{g}_i(\bm{\theta}_i^{(k)})\|},
  \label{eq:adapt}
\end{equation}
with step size $\alpha = 0.3\,$m.  The gradient is approximated via
centred finite differences of step $h = 0.1\,$m.  Normalization
ensures step-size invariance across widely varying signal magnitudes.

\paragraph{Combine.}
A single weighted average consensus step follows:
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\theta}_i^{(k+1)} = (1-\beta)\,\bm{\theta}_i^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}
  + \frac{\beta}{|\mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{T}|}
    \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i \cap \mathcal{T}} \bm{\theta}_j^{(k+\frac{1}{2})},
  \label{eq:combine}
\end{equation}
with $\beta = 0.4$.  This implements the Adapt-then-Combine (ATC)
diffusion strategy~\cite{chen2012diffusion}.

\subsection{Min-Consensus Partner Selection}

When the first TRACK drone $h$ reaches the STOP state (strong signal
threshold), the system must select the $P = 2$ nearest reachable
drones as support partners in a fully distributed manner.

\paragraph{Step 0 — Connected component.}
A BFS from $h$ identifies $\mathcal{C}(h)$, the set of drones
reachable through $\mathcal{G}$.  Drones in
$\mathcal{V} \setminus \mathcal{C}(h)$ are excluded.

\paragraph{Step 1 — Initialisation.}
Each $i \in \mathcal{C}(h)$ maintains a distance row
$\mathbf{D}_i \in (\mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\})^{|\mathcal{C}(h)|}$,
initialised to $+\infty$ everywhere except $\mathbf{D}_h[h] = 0$.

\paragraph{Step 2 — Iterations.}
For up to $K_{\max} = 10$ rounds, two updates propagate synchronously:
(i)~\emph{awareness}: newly-informed drones set their own distance
entry $\mathbf{D}_i[i] = \|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_h\|$;
(ii)~\emph{min-update}: $\mathbf{D}_i \leftarrow
\min(\mathbf{D}_i, \mathbf{D}_j)$ for each neighbour $j$.  The
algorithm converges in at most
$\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}[\mathcal{C}(h)])$ rounds.

\paragraph{Step 3 — Selection.}
The agreed distance for candidate $c_k$ is
$d^*_k = \min_{i \in \mathcal{C}(h)} \mathbf{D}_i[k]$.
The $P$ partners with smallest $d^*_k$ are selected.

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Noise Calibration}

Before flight, each drone collects $M = 20$ ARTVA measurements at a
known background location and estimates its local noise standard
deviation $\sigma_i$.  Average consensus (10 iterations) drives all
agents to a common estimate $\hat{\sigma}$, which sets:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Detection threshold}: $\tau_{\mathrm{det}} =
        100\,\hat{\sigma}$\ \ (transition SEARCH $\to$ TRACK);
  \item \textbf{Stop threshold}: $\tau_{\mathrm{stop}} =
        1000\,\hat{\sigma}$\ \ (transition TRACK/SUPPORT $\to$ STOP).
\end{itemize}
For the nominal noise level $\sigma = 10^{-6}$, these yield
$\tau_{\mathrm{det}} \approx 10^{-4}$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{stop}} \approx 10^{-3}$, which correspond to typical
signal strengths at distances of roughly $20\,$m and $5\,$m from the
source, respectively.  Both factors are design parameters in
\texttt{config.py} and scale automatically with the measured noise.

\subsection{Mission State Machine}

Each drone operates a finite state machine with four states:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{Search}: lawnmower boustrophedon coverage of an
        assigned workspace lane; the area is divided equally among
        $N$ drones along the $x$-axis.
  \item \textsc{Track}: DCGD active; the drone explores three
        candidate waypoints ($0°$, $+60°$, $-60°$ from current
        heading) to climb the signal gradient.
  \item \textsc{Stop}: strong-signal hovering; triggers partner
        selection and orbit assignment.
  \item \textsc{Support}: circular orbit (CW or CCW) around the
        STOP drone at the estimated source distance, providing
        additional measurement positions for DCGD.
\end{itemize}
All state transitions are arrival-gated: waypoint updates are issued
only when the current target is within $\delta = 0.3\,$m of the
estimated drone position.  State-transition waypoints are set
immediately.

\subsection{Refinement Phase}

When $\geq 3$ drones reach \textsc{Stop}, the DCGD runs an additional
$K_{\mathrm{refine}} = 300$ iterations without drone movement.  This
stationary refinement exploits the dense measurement batch accumulated
near the source to sharpen the estimate before reporting.

\subsection{Cooperative Localization}

Figure~\ref{fig:imdcl_error} shows the position estimation error for a
representative drone over a 500-step simulation with $N = 5$ drones.
In the absence of absolute positioning (GPS-denied scenario), the IMDCL
filter combines inter-drone relative measurements
($\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0.3\,$m) and LiDAR altitude corrections
($\sigma_{\mathrm{LiDAR}} = 0.05\,$m) to maintain a bounded RMS
position error below $0.5\,$m.  The cross-covariance correction terms
in IMDCL prevent information double-counting that would otherwise
inflate the filter's confidence.

\subsection{Parametric Experimental Sweep}
\label{sec:sweep}

To assess robustness across diverse operating conditions we run a
comprehensive batch sweep using the \texttt{run\_experiments.py} script.
Each experiment is an independent simulation; results are recorded in
a CSV file with automatic resume after interruption.

\subsubsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Terrain.}
Five distinct workspace patches of size $A \times A$ metres are
extracted from the real TINItaly 10\,m-resolution DEM
(tile \texttt{w51065\_s10.tif}, Trentino Alps).  The patch centres are
distributed across the tile at row/column fractions
$(0.50, 0.50)$, $(0.35, 0.45)$, $(0.45, 0.55)$, $(0.60, 0.45)$, and
$(0.60, 0.58)$, yielding genuinely different alpine terrain profiles
(ridgelines, bowls, gentle slopes).

\paragraph{Victim placement.}
Four deterministic relative positions
$(0.25, 0.25)$, $(0.50, 0.50)$, $(0.75, 0.50)$, $(0.35, 0.70)$
cover the four quadrants of the workspace.  Four additional victim
positions are drawn pseudo-randomly with a fixed seed
($s_{\mathrm{grid}} = 2024$) to widen coverage without sacrificing
reproducibility.  Burial depth is similarly mixed: three fixed values
($1.0$, $3.0$, $5.0\,$m) plus three randomly sampled depths in
$[0.5, 4.5]\,$m.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Each run records: (i)~\emph{found} (Boolean: $\geq 3$ drones in STOP
within 15\,min); (ii)~\emph{time\_found} [s]; (iii)~\emph{pos\_std}
[m]: square root of the sum of per-axis variance of the drones'
individual source estimates (inter-drone consensus quality);
(iv)~\emph{est\_error\_2d} [m]: 2-D Euclidean error between the mean
estimate and the true victim XY position.

\subsubsection{Representative Results}

End-to-end mission results for the nominal configuration ($N = 5$,
$A = 200\,$m, $R_c = 80\,$m, $\sigma = 10^{-7}$, centre terrain
patch) are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item mean time to first STOP event: $\approx 320$ steps
        ($= 32\,$s simulated time);
  \item final 2-D source localization error after refinement:
        $0.42 \pm 0.18\,$m (mean $\pm$ std over the 8 victim
        positions);
  \item zero partner-selection failures in all connected-graph
        scenarios ($R_c \geq 50\,$m).
\end{itemize}

Performance degrades gracefully as $R_c$ decreases: at $R_c = 25\,$m
the network becomes sparse and partner selection occasionally fails,
increasing the mean localization error by approximately $0.3\,$m.
Larger fleet sizes ($N = 5$ vs.\ $N = 3$) consistently reduce both
search time and estimation error thanks to better DCGD consensus.
Burial depth has the strongest effect on time-to-detect (deeper
victims produce weaker signals), while the terrain patch has minor
impact on localization accuracy once the drone reaches TRACK state.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This report presented a fully distributed multi-drone system for ARTVA
victim localization integrating four cooperative algorithms: MPC
path-following, IMDCL cooperative localization, DCGD source
estimation, and min-consensus partner selection.  All components
operate with only local state and within a limited communication
radius, requiring no central coordinator.

A comprehensive parametric sweep over 12\,960 conditions---spanning
five real alpine terrain patches, randomized victim positions and
burial depths, varying fleet sizes, noise levels, and communication
radii---demonstrates sub-meter source localization accuracy and robust
partner selection across diverse operating conditions.

Key limitations include sensitivity of DCGD to the initial estimate
placement and the assumption of a single buried source.

Future directions include: (i)~multi-source extension via clustering;
(ii)~3-D terrain-aware path planning for steeper terrain;
(iii)~replacement of finite-difference gradients with analytic
Jacobians of~\eqref{eq:artva} to reduce noise sensitivity; and
(iv)~experimental validation on physical drone platforms.
