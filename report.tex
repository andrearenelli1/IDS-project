\begin{abstract}
When an avalanche strikes, survival odds collapse within minutes---and
every second of search counts.
We present a fully distributed multi-drone system that localizes a
buried ARTVA beacon with no central coordinator and no
external positioning: each drone acts on local state alone and talks
only to peers within radio range.
The entire system is evaluated under full GPS denial---the worst-case
scenario for self-localization---where each drone relies solely on
onboard inertial integration corrected by cooperative UWB ranging and
LiDAR altitude (IMDCL).  This is a deliberate stress test: a single GPS-equipped drone,
on average connected to the fleet, suffices to propagate an absolute
fix to all agents without any architectural change.
Each agent runs a five-state machine---\textsc{Search},
\textsc{Track}, \textsc{Stop}, \textsc{Support}, \textsc{Final\,Orbit}---driven by an MPC
controller, with Extremum Seeking guiding it up the signal gradient and
a distributed Particle Filter fusing every nearby measurement into a
sharp 3-D victim estimate.
The system is evaluated through a parametric sweep of $12420$ runs on real
alpine terrains, spanning fleet size, ARTVA noise, communication radius, and
burial depth, assessing not only the localization error but also the
inter-drone consistency of the distributed estimate via confidence-ellipse
metrics.  Over successful runs it delivers a median planar localization
error of $0.73$\,m---well within operational requirements---while
remaining essentially invariant to the ARTVA noise level across three
decades thanks to adaptive thresholds.  Success rate scales
with fleet size and workspace: 3 drones suffice on a $100\times100$\,m area
($95.6\%$), while at least 5 are needed to reach comparable reliability on
$200\times200$\,m ($90.7\%$ vs.\ $73.9\%$ with 3).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Avalanche burial is one of the most time-critical rescue scenarios:
survival probability exceeds 90\,\% in the first 15 minutes and drops
to below 30\,\% after 35 minutes~\cite{Falk1994}.
The ARTVA (Apparecchio di Ricerca dei Travolti in Valanga) standard
transmits a 457\,kHz magnetic field; a drone fleet equipped with
receiving antennas can sweep the burial zone far faster than human
searchers, and we target a $600$\,s mission budget to stay well within
the critical survival window.

The core challenge is to coordinate multiple UAVs without a central
server, localize a buried beacon from its weak magnetic dipole signal,
and deliver a precise 3-D position estimate to ground rescuers---all
under GPS denial (avalanche terrain may block satellite signals) and
tight real-time constraints.  Once the fleet localizes the source, a drone lands on the estimated
burial point; the operationally relevant error is the distance from
that touchdown to the true victim (formalized in Sec.~\ref{sec:pf}).
We deliberately target the worst case---full GPS denial---where
each drone's position would otherwise drift unbounded as odometric error
accumulates; the cooperative IMDCL filter mitigates this by fusing
inter-drone UWB ranging and LiDAR altitude, and degrades gracefully
whenever a GPS fix becomes available (Sec.~\ref{sec:imdcl}).

\subsection*{Problem Formulation}

Given a team of $N$ UAVs deployed over an alpine search area with
unknown victim position $\bm{\theta} = [x_v, y_v, z_v]^\top$, the
goal is to:
(i) detect the ARTVA beacon as quickly as possible,
(ii) cooperatively estimate $\bm{\theta}$ within a target accuracy
sufficient for ground rescuers to locate the burial point, and
(iii) do so without any central coordinator, operating only with
local state and bounded peer-to-peer communication under GPS denial.

This work contributes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A five-state FSM with waypoint management,
        combining a coverage lawnmower (\textsc{Search}), Extremum
        Seeking source tracking (\textsc{Track}), cooperative hover
        (\textsc{Stop}), cooperative orbit (\textsc{Support}), and a
        multi-view final orbit (\textsc{Final\,Orbit}).
  \item Dynamic detection thresholds with a physics-based floor,
        derived at runtime from a distributed noise calibration step.
  \item A 3-D cooperative Particle Filter for victim localization,
        initialized from the ARTVA dipole model in polar coordinates,
        constrained below the LiDAR-sensed terrain surface, and updated
        cooperatively from all drones in range.
  \item Full simulation over real satellite-scanned terrain of the
        Trentino Alps, with a parametric sweep across terrain patches,
        fleet sizes, noise levels, and communication radii.
\end{itemize}

\section{Adopted Models}
\label{sec:models}

\subsection{Communication System}
\label{sec:comm}

The drone network is modeled as a time-varying undirected graph
$\mathcal{G}(t) = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}(t))$, where
$\mathcal{V} = \{1, \ldots, N\}$ is the set of agents and
$(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}(t)$ if and only if
$\|\mathbf{p}_i(t) - \mathbf{p}_j(t)\| < r_c$.
All inter-agent communication is peer-to-peer within the communication radius $r_c$.

Two distributed protocols run over this graph:

\paragraph{Average-consensus (noise calibration)}
At startup, each drone takes $N_{\text{cal}} = 20$ ARTVA samples at
its initial position.  An average-consensus protocol over
$\mathcal{G}(0)$ aggregates all measurements into a team-wide noise
estimate $(\hat{\mu}, \hat{\sigma})$ before any flight begins.

\paragraph{Min-consensus (partner selection)}
When a drone $h$ enters \textsc{Stop}, a BFS from $h$ first identifies
the connected component $\mathcal{C}(h)$ of reachable drones; agents
outside it are excluded.  A min-consensus flood then propagates
synchronously for up to $k_{\max} = 10$ rounds: newly-informed drones
record their distance $\|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i - \hat{\mathbf{p}}_h\|$ and
each applies a min-update over its neighbors.  Convergence is guaranteed
in at most $\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{G}[\mathcal{C}(h)])$ rounds, after
which the two closest available drones are recruited as \textsc{Support}
partners.

UWB radio is used for ranging (inter-drone distance measurements fed
into IMDCL), for piggy-backing lightweight state messages
(position estimate, ARTVA reading, FSM state, IMDCL messages).

\subsection{System Model}
\label{sec:sysmodel}

\subsubsection{Drone dynamics}

Since the goal of this work is a proof of concept for the whole
distributed framework, each drone is modeled as a 3-D point mass
controlled in acceleration.  This abstraction is justified on two
grounds: first, the ARTVA signal depends only on position and not on
orientation, so attitude dynamics do not affect the measurement model;
second, the point-mass model can be treated as a black box hiding a
lower-level controller that, given the desired acceleration from the
high-level MPC, computes the actual motor commands, as is standard in
quadrotor control architectures.  States and inputs are:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x} = [p_x, p_y, p_z, v_x, v_y, v_z]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6,
  \quad
  \mathbf{u} = [a_x, a_y, a_z]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3
  \label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
subject to acceleration bound $|\mathbf{a}| \leq 6$\,m/s$^2$ and
speed bound $|\mathbf{v}| \leq 3$\,m/s.

The continuous dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A_c \mathbf{x} + B_c
\mathbf{u}$ are discretized with a Zero-Order Hold (step
$\Delta t = 0.1$\,s), assuming $\mathbf{u}$ constant over each interval.
The matrix exponential resolves in closed form, yielding an exact
discretization:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}(k+1) = F\,\mathbf{x}(k) + G\,\mathbf{u}(k) +
  G\,\bm{\eta}(k)
  \label{eq:zoh}
\end{equation}
where $\bm{\eta} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q)$ is isotropic process noise
with standard deviation $\sigma_a$, and
\begin{equation}
  F = \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & \Delta t\,I_3 \\ 0_3 & I_3 \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  G = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t^2 I_3 \\ \Delta t\,I_3
  \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  Q = \sigma_a I_3.
\end{equation}

The drone targets $h_{\text{AGL}} = 1.5$\,m above ground to maximise
signal strength; terrain height is read from elevation data at runtime.

\subsubsection{ARTVA signal model}

The buried transmitter is modeled as a magnetic dipole aligned with
gravity.  The received power at position $\mathbf{p}$ from a source at
$\bm{\theta} = [x_v, y_v, z_v]^\top$ is
\begin{equation}
  S(\mathbf{p}) = \frac{m\,\sqrt{1 + 3\cos^2\!\psi}}{\|\mathbf{p} -
  \bm{\theta}\|^3}
  \label{eq:artva}
\end{equation}
where $m$ is the normalized magnetic moment and $\cos\psi =
-(\mathbf{p} - \bm{\theta})_z / \|\mathbf{p} - \bm{\theta}\|$.
An additive Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_n^2)$ is
superimposed.  Signal measurements are low-pass filtered with a sliding
batch window of $N_{\text{sig}} = 5$ samples to reduce noise influence
on state transitions.

The dipole moment $m$ is a dimensionless scaling factor that sets the
unit of $S$; since all signal thresholds and noise levels are expressed
in the same units, its absolute value does not affect system behaviour
(we take $m = 1$).
The transmitter is assumed aligned with the vertical axis; a
non-vertical dipole shifts the signal maximum laterally~\cite{Azzollini2021},
but this is not a limitation: any strategy converges to the maximum,
which is the same reference point a hand-held ARTVA search provides,
and the true position is refined manually.

Each drone cycles through five states (Fig.~\ref{fig:fsm}).
In \textsc{Search} it sweeps a lawnmower strip; on detecting
$S \ge \tau_{\text{detect}}$ it enters \textsc{Track}, where
Extremum Seeking drives it toward the signal maximum while the
Particle Filter is initialised.  Reaching $\tau_{\text{stop}}$, it
enters \textsc{Stop} and hovers, recruiting two partners via
min-consensus.  Each partner---drawn from any drone in
\textsc{Search} or \textsc{Track}---transitions to \textsc{Support}
and orbits the \textsc{Stop} drone on a circle of radius
$r_{\text{orbit}}$ (one CW, one CCW), sized to cross $\tau_{\text{stop}}$
and gather diverse angular measurements for the PF.

When all PF-active drones have reached \textsc{Stop}, each flies
a single \textsc{Final\,Orbit} of $N_{\text{wp}}$ waypoints around
the frozen estimate---a multi-view sweep essential for depth
estimation.  Success is assessed on the PF output
(Sec.~\ref{sec:pf}) within the $600$\,s operational deadline.

\subsection{Dynamic Detection Thresholds}
\label{sec:thresholds}

Fixed thresholds would cause problems across noise levels: under high
noise the drone could trigger on a distorted signal and ES would track
noise rather than the true gradient, while under low noise an early
trigger far from the source would demand convergence from a large
initial distance, slowing the approach despite a clean signal.
Using the team-wide noise estimate from consensus, the thresholds are:
\begin{align}
  \tau_{\text{detect}} &= \max\!\left(
    \hat{\mu} + 5\hat{\sigma},\;
    \frac{m}{r_{\text{ES}}^3}
  \right)
  \label{eq:detect_thr}\\
  \tau_{\text{stop}} &= \frac{m}{r_{\text{found}}^3}
  \label{eq:stop_thr}
\end{align}
where $r_{\text{ES}} = 50$\,m is the maximum reliable ES convergence
radius for the selected parameters ~\cite{Azzollini2021} and $r_{\text{found}} = 10$\,m is the
target localization accuracy.  The floor prevents detection from triggering too far from
the source under low noise, while the $5\hat{\sigma}$ term adapts the
threshold to the measured noise level.  The stop threshold is purely
geometric, relying on $\tau_{\text{detect}}$ as an upstream noise gate.

\subsection{Search: Adaptive Lawnmower}
\label{sec:lawnmower}

In \textsc{Search}, each drone covers a vertical strip of the workspace
via a back-and-forth lawnmower pattern.  Lane spacing is derived at runtime from
$\tau_{\text{detect}}$:
\begin{equation}
  r_{\text{detect}} = \left(\frac{m}{\tau_{\text{detect}}}\right)^{1/3},
  \quad
  n_{\text{lanes}} = \left\lceil \frac{W/N_d}{2\,r_{\text{detect}}}
  \right\rceil
  \label{eq:lanes}
\end{equation}
where $W$ is the workspace width and $N_d$ the number of drones.
A lower noise level produces a larger range of detection and hence
fewer, wider lanes, reducing search time proportionally.
Lanes are assigned with stride $N_d$ (drone $i$ covers lanes $i,\,
i{+}N_d,\, i{+}2N_d, \ldots$), so that active drones always sweep
adjacent lanes, maximizing communication-graph connectivity throughout
the search phase and keeping neighbors in range for cooperative IMDCL
and PF updates.

\subsection{Track: Extremum Seeking}
\label{sec:es}

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\columnwidth]{figures/ES_example.png}
    \caption{ES trajectory example, image adapted from~\cite{Azzollini2021}.}
    \label{fig:ESexample}
\end{figure}

Upon detection, the drone switches to Extremum Seeking
(ES)~\cite{Azzollini2021}, a model-free algorithm that drives a
circular probe trajectory whose center gradient-ascends toward the
signal maximum.  Following equations (11)--(13) of~\cite{Azzollini2021}:
\begin{align}
  \dot{x}_{\text{ref}} &= \sqrt{\alpha\omega}\,\cos(\omega t +
  \kappa\,y_t) \label{eq:es_x}\\
  \dot{y}_{\text{ref}} &= \sqrt{\alpha\omega}\,\sin(\omega t +
  \kappa\,y_t) \label{eq:es_y}\\
  \dot{\alpha} &= \frac{\alpha_{\max} - \alpha}{\lambda}
  \label{eq:es_alpha}
\end{align}
where $y_t = 1/\!\sqrt[3]{S}$ is the conditioned signal (convex with
minimum at source), $\alpha$ ramps from 0 to $\alpha_{\max}=20$ with
time constant $\lambda = 15$\,s, and $\omega = V_{\max}^2/\alpha_{\max}
= 0.45$\,rad/s ensures $\sqrt{\alpha_{\max}\omega} = V_{\max} = 3$\,m/s.

\paragraph{Convergence and parameter choice}
The ES centre is guaranteed to converge to within a residual
neighbourhood of radius $\propto 1/\omega$ around the source---a bound
known as $(1/\omega)$-SPUUB (Semi-Practical Uniform Ultimate
Boundedness)~\cite{Azzollini2021}.  The domain of attraction can be
made arbitrarily large by increasing $\kappa\alpha_{\max}$, at the cost
of slower convergence.  We adopt $\alpha_{\max}=20$ and lower $\omega$
from $0.65$ to $0.45$\,rad/s (and $\kappa$ from $0.07$ to $0.05$) to
satisfy our velocity ceiling of $3$\,m/s, with $r_{\text{ES}}=50$\,m
as the empirically validated convergence range~\cite{Azzollini2021}.

\subsection{Stop and Support: Cooperative Confirmation}
\label{sec:stop_support}
When a drone's signal reaches $\tau_{\text{stop}}$, it enters
\textsc{Stop} and recruits two partners via min-consensus.
Each partner orbits the calling drone at radius $r_{\text{orbit}}$,
computed from eq.~\eqref{eq:artva} as the estimated distance to the
source under a spherical-symmetry approximation:
\begin{equation}
  r_{\text{orbit}} = \left(\frac{m}{S_{\text{stop}}}\right)^{1/3}
  \label{eq:orbit_r}
\end{equation}
This places the orbit approximately over the source, ensuring the
support drone passes through $\tau_{\text{stop}}$ and the
\textsc{Support}$\to$\textsc{Stop} transition is virtually guaranteed.
One partner orbits clockwise and one counter-clockwise (start angles
$0$ and $\pi$), ensuring diverse angular measurements for the PF.

\subsection{Distributed Source Estimation: Particle Filter}
\label{sec:pf}

When a drone transitions to \textsc{Track} (or to \textsc{Support} without
prior detection), it initializes a 3-D Particle Filter with $N_p = 300$
particles $\{\bm{\xi}_k\}_{k=1}^{N_p}$ and uniform weights $w_k = 1/N_p$.

\paragraph{LiDAR ground constraint}
The victim is buried, so every particle must lie \emph{below} the terrain
surface.  The LiDAR altimeter provides the terrain height directly beneath
the drone, $z_{\text{ground}} = p_z - h_{\text{AGL}}$, and under the
locally-flat assumption this yields a hard physical prior on every particle:
\begin{equation}
  \xi_{k,z} \leq z_{\text{ground}} = p_z - h_{\text{AGL}}.
  \label{eq:pf_ground}
\end{equation}
The constraint is enforced both at initialization and after every
resampling, so that the particle cloud never occupies the
physically impossible volume above the snow surface.

\paragraph{Initialization}
Particles are drawn in polar coordinates relative to the drone's
estimated position $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$, where $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$ is the
azimuth and $\psi\in[0,\pi/2)$ the polar angle from the vertical
(so $\psi=0$ means directly below the drone).  Given the first
measurement $S_0$, the shell radius depends only on $\psi$:
\begin{equation}
  r_0(\psi) = \left(\frac{m\sqrt{1+3\cos^2\!\psi}}{S_0}\right)^{1/3}.
  \label{eq:pf_r0}
\end{equation}
The ground constraint~\eqref{eq:pf_ground} requires $r_0(\psi)\cos\psi
\geq h_{\text{AGL}}$; since the left-hand side is monotonically
decreasing in $\sin\psi$, this admits a unique upper bound:
\begin{equation}
  \sin\psi_{\max} = \sup\bigl\{\sin\psi :
  r_0(\psi)\cos\psi \geq h_{\text{AGL}}\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:sinpsimax}
\end{equation}
We sample $\sin\psi$ uniformly
rather than $\psi$ directly to obtain an isotropic distribution over
the hemisphere.  For each particle $k$:
\begin{gather}
  \phi_k \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 2\pi],\quad
  \sin\psi_k \sim \mathcal{U}[0,\,\sin\psi_{\max}],\notag\\
  r_{0,k} = \left(\frac{m\sqrt{1+3(1-\sin^2\!\psi_k)}}{S_0}\right)^{1/3},
  \label{eq:pf_init_polar}
\end{gather}
converted to Cartesian coordinates as
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\xi}_k = \hat{\mathbf{p}} + r_{0,k}
  \begin{bmatrix} \cos\phi_k\,\sin\psi_k \\ \sin\phi_k\,\sin\psi_k \\
  -\cos\psi_k \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:pf_init_cart}
\end{equation}
Since $r_{0,k}$ depends only on $\psi_k$, the azimuthal distribution
remains uniform after initialization, as expected from a single measurement.

\paragraph{Weight update}
At each step the predicted signal at particle $k$ for the measuring
drone at position $\mathbf{p}$ is
$S_{\text{pred},k} = S(\mathbf{p}, \bm{\xi}_k)$ from~\eqref{eq:artva},
and the weight is updated with the ARTVA likelihood:
\begin{equation}
  w_k \leftarrow w_k \cdot \exp\!\left(
    -\frac{(S - S_{\text{pred},k})^2}{2\,\sigma_{\text{eff}}^2}
  \right),
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
with the adaptive noise model
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{\hat{\sigma}^2 + (0.20\,S)^2},
  \label{eq:sigma_eff}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\sigma}$ is the team-wide noise estimate from the
pre-flight consensus calibration (Section~\ref{sec:comm}), and the
20\,\% multiplicative term handles the dynamic range of cooperative
updates: drones orbiting near the source receive signals orders of
magnitude larger than distant drones, and a fixed $\hat{\sigma}$
alone would collapse all weights to zero on cross-drone updates.  Weights are
then normalized:
\begin{equation}
  w_k \leftarrow \frac{w_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{N_p} w_j}.
  \label{eq:pf_norm}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Resampling}
Systematic resampling is triggered only when the effective particle count
\begin{equation}
  N_{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N_p} w_k^2}
  \label{eq:neff}
\end{equation}
drops below $N_p/2$, preserving particle diversity near the source.
Systematic resampling draws a single offset $u \sim \mathcal{U}(0,
1/N_p)$ and selects particles at the $N_p$ evenly-spaced positions
$\{(i + u)/N_p\}_{i=0}^{N_p-1}$ along the weight CDF, so that each
particle is replicated exactly $\lfloor w_k N_p \rfloor$ or $\lceil
w_k N_p \rceil$ times.  Compared to multinomial resampling, the shared
offset guarantees full coverage of $[0,1]$ with minimal variance.
Weights are then reset to $1/N_p$.

After resampling, independent Gaussian jitter is added to each particle:
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\xi}_k \leftarrow \bm{\xi}_k +
  \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\,\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\sigma}_j^2)),
  \label{eq:pf_jitter}
\end{equation}
with $\bm{\sigma}_j = \max(0.1\,\hat{\bm{\sigma}},\,[1,1,0.5]^{\top})$\,m,
where $\hat{\bm{\sigma}}$ is the per-axis standard deviation of the current
particle cloud, preventing collapse when the cloud is already concentrated.
Since vertical jitter can push particles above the terrain, the ground
constraint~\eqref{eq:pf_ground} is re-enforced by reflecting any violating
particle back below the surface:
\begin{equation}
  \xi_{k,z} \leftarrow 2\,z_{\text{ground}} - \xi_{k,z}
  \quad \text{if } \xi_{k,z} > z_{\text{ground}},
  \label{eq:pf_reflect}
\end{equation}
which preserves particle density immediately beneath the surface, unlike
a clamp that would collapse mass exactly at $z_{\text{ground}}$.

\paragraph{Cooperative fusion}
Any drone $i$ with an active PF incorporates the ARTVA measurements of
all neighbors that also have an active PF and lie within $r_c$:
\begin{equation}
  \forall j \neq i : \|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j\| < r_c
  \;\wedge\; \mathrm{PF}_j \text{ active}
  \;\Rightarrow\; \text{apply \eqref{eq:weight} with }
  (\mathbf{p}_j, S_j),
  \label{eq:pf_fusion}
\end{equation}
fusing diverse viewpoints without exchanging particle sets.  The source
estimate is the weighted mean of all particles:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bm{\theta}}_i = \sum_{k=1}^{N_p} w_k\,\bm{\xi}_k.
  \label{eq:pf_estimate}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Estimate uncertainty and inter-drone agreement}
The point estimate~\eqref{eq:pf_estimate} alone does not convey how
\emph{confident} each drone is, nor whether the drones \emph{agree}.
We therefore characterise each drone's planar uncertainty by the
weighted $2\times2$ covariance of its particle cloud,
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\Sigma}_i = \sum_{k=1}^{N_p} w_k\,
  \bigl(\bm{\xi}_k^{xy} - \hat{\bm{\theta}}_i^{xy}\bigr)
  \bigl(\bm{\xi}_k^{xy} - \hat{\bm{\theta}}_i^{xy}\bigr)^{\!\top},
  \label{eq:pf_cov}
\end{equation}
whose $\alpha$-level confidence ellipse is the set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_i = \Bigl\{\, \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^2 :
  (\mathbf{x}-\hat{\bm{\theta}}_i^{xy})^{\!\top}\bm{\Sigma}_i^{-1}
  (\mathbf{x}-\hat{\bm{\theta}}_i^{xy}) \le k^2 \,\Bigr\},
  \label{eq:pf_ellipse}
\end{equation}
with $k^2 = \chi^2_{2}(\alpha) = -2\ln(1-\alpha)$ the chi-square quantile
for two degrees of freedom (closed form for $2$ d.o.f.;
$\alpha = 0.95 \Rightarrow k^2 \approx 5.991$).  The area of this ellipse,
\begin{equation}
  A_i = \pi\,k^2\sqrt{\det \bm{\Sigma}_i},
  \label{eq:pf_area}
\end{equation}
measures the \emph{intra-drone} uncertainty: a smaller $A_i$ means a more
concentrated, more confident estimate.  Averaging over the $D$ drones with
an active PF gives the mean confidence-ellipse area $\bar{A} =
\frac{1}{D}\sum_{i} A_i$.

To quantify \emph{inter-drone} agreement we use the
intersection-over-union (IoU) of the confidence ellipses of every drone
pair $(i,j)$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{IoU}_{ij} =
  \frac{\operatorname{area}(\mathcal{E}_i \cap \mathcal{E}_j)}
       {\operatorname{area}(\mathcal{E}_i \cup \mathcal{E}_j)}
  \in [0,1],
  \label{eq:pf_iou}
\end{equation}
computed exactly by convex-polygon clipping of the two ellipse contours,
and report its pairwise mean
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\mathrm{IoU}} = \frac{2}{D(D-1)}
  \sum_{i<j} \mathrm{IoU}_{ij}.
  \label{eq:pf_iou_mean}
\end{equation}
Normalising by the union removes the bias of larger ellipses trivially
overlapping more, and the IoU jointly captures both centre disagreement
and per-drone uncertainty.  The ideal regime is \emph{small} $\bar{A}$
(confident) with \emph{high} $\overline{\mathrm{IoU}}$ (mutually consistent).

\paragraph{Success criterion}
Under GPS denial the filter operates entirely in the drone's
\emph{estimated} frame: every particle and the resulting estimate
$\hat{\bm{\theta}}_i$ are expressed relative to the IMDCL self-position
$\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i$, not the (unknown) true position $\mathbf{p}_i$.
This distinction has a direct operational meaning: when drone $i$ is
commanded to land on $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_i$, it navigates relative to
its own believed position $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i$, so the actual touchdown
point in world coordinates is $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_i -
(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i - \mathbf{p}_i)$---the estimate shifted by the
IMDCL drift.  It is at this point that rescuers begin excavation, so
this drift-corrected quantity is what must be accurate.  Formally:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bm{\theta}}_i^{\,\mathrm{w}} =
  \hat{\bm{\theta}}_i - (\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i - \mathbf{p}_i),
  \label{eq:drift_correct}
\end{equation}
and it is $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_i^{\,\mathrm{w}}$ against which success is
assessed.  A run is declared \emph{successful} when at least one drone
with an active PF has a drift-corrected confidence planar ellipse that
(i) \emph{contains} the true victim,
and (ii) is sufficiently concentrated, $A_i \le A_{\max}$.  A single drone
is therefore enough to localize the source.  We set the confidence level
to $95\%$ and $A_{\max} = \pi\,(5\,\mathrm{m})^2 \approx 78.5\,\mathrm{m}^2$
(a circle of radius $\approx 5$\,m), consistent with the practical search
box of~\cite{Azzollini2021}.

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Software Architecture}

The system is implemented as independent Python modules;
\texttt{config.py} is the single source of truth for all parameters.

\subsection{Flight Controller: MPC}
\label{sec:mpc}

Each drone tracks its mission waypoints via a finite-horizon MPC with
horizon $N = 20$ steps and $\Delta t = 0.1$\,s.  At every control step
the current IMDCL estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}^+_i$ serves as the initial
condition, and the solver minimizes a quadratic tracking cost over the
predicted input sequence $\mathbf{u}_0, \ldots, \mathbf{u}_{N-1}$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \min_{\mathbf{u}_0, \ldots, \mathbf{u}_{N-1}}\;
  &\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \Big( w_p \|\mathbf{p}_k - \mathbf{p}_{\text{ref}}\|^2
    + w_a \|\mathbf{u}_k\|^2 \Big) \\
  &\quad + w_{fp} \|\mathbf{p}_N - \mathbf{p}_{\text{ref}}\|^2
    + w_{fv} \|\mathbf{v}_N\|^2
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:mpc_cost}
\end{equation}
subject to the discrete dynamics~\eqref{eq:zoh} and the box constraints
\begin{equation}
  \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_\infty \leq v_{\max} = 3\,\text{m/s},\quad
  \|\mathbf{u}_k\|_\infty \leq a_{\max} = 6\,\text{m/s}^2,
  \label{eq:mpc_constraints}
\end{equation}
for all $k = 0, \ldots, N$.  Here $\mathbf{p}_k, \mathbf{v}_k$ are the
position and velocity components of the predicted state
$\mathbf{x}_k$, $\mathbf{p}_{\text{ref}}$ is the active reference
waypoint, and $(w_p, w_a, w_{fp}, w_{fv})$ are the stage-position,
control-effort, terminal-position, and terminal-velocity weights,
respectively.  The terminal velocity penalty $w_{fv}\|\mathbf{v}_N\|^2$
discourages overshoot at the waypoint.
The MPC always uses the IMDCL state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$,
never the true state, making it robust to GPS denial by design.
The target altitude is set from the terrain as
$z_{\text{tgt}} = z_{\text{terrain}}(x, y) + h_{\text{AGL}}$, keeping
the drone at a constant 1.5\,m above the terrain surface.
The problem is solved with the IPOPT interior-point solver through the
CasADi symbolic framework.

\paragraph{Warm-starting}
At mission start a single initialization solve runs up to
$N_{\text{init}} = 1000$ IPOPT iterations to obtain a high-quality
primal--dual solution.  All subsequent steps warm-start from the
previous solution: the optimal state trajectory is shifted by one step
to initialize the primal variables, and IPOPT is then limited to
$N_{\text{mpc}} = 3$ iterations per step, keeping the average solve time
below 5\,ms.

\paragraph{Waypoint sequencing}
When the estimated distance to the active waypoint drops below
$\delta = 0.3$\,m, the planner advances to the next target and updates
the reference without triggering a new initialization solve; the
shifted warm-start carries over, ensuring smooth transitions between
consecutive waypoints.

\subsection{GPS-Denied Localization: IMDCL}
\label{sec:imdcl}

In GPS-denied conditions each drone runs an instance of the IMDCL
filter~\cite{Kia2016}, a tightly-coupled distributed cooperative
localization algorithm that replicates the result of a centralized EKF
without all-to-all communication at every step.
The key insight is that the cross-covariance terms between agents $i$
and $j$, while never stored globally, can be reconstructed locally
whenever a relative measurement occurs.  To this end each agent
maintains four quantities: the state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i$,
the error covariance $\mathbf{P}_i$ (initialized with position standard
deviation $\sqrt{P^{pp}_0} = 0.5$\,m), the cumulative Jacobian product
$\Phi_i$ (initialized to $I$ and multiplied by $F$ at each propagation
step), and local copies of the cross-covariance seed matrices
$\{\Pi^i_{jl}\}$.  The cross-covariance is then recovered as
$P_{ij}(k) = \Phi_i(k)\,\Pi^i_{ij}\,\Phi_j(k)^\top$ without additional
communication.

At each step the filter executes the following operations.

\paragraph{Propagation (every step)}
Every agent independently propagates its local filter and advances
$\Phi_i$, with no inter-agent communication:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\mathbf{x}}^-_i(k{+}1) &= F\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}^+_i(k) +
  G\,\mathbf{u}_i(k), \\
  \mathbf{P}^-_i(k{+}1) &= F\,\mathbf{P}^+_i(k)\,F^\top +
  G\,Q\,G^\top, \\
  \Phi_i(k{+}1) &= F\,\Phi_i(k).
  \label{eq:imdcl_prop}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Absolute update (LiDAR altitude)}
The LiDAR altimeter provides an absolute altitude measurement
($\sigma_{\text{LiDAR}} = 0.05$\,m), processed as a standard EKF
correction on the $p_z$ component.

\paragraph{Relative update (agent $a$ measures agent $b$)}
When agent $a$ obtains a UWB relative-position measurement
$z_{ab} = \mathbf{p}_b - \mathbf{p}_a + \nu_{ab}$ of an in-range
neighbor $b$ ($\sigma_{\text{rel}} = 0.3$\,m), it is declared the
interim master.  It requests the landmark message
$(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^-_b, \Phi_b, \mathbf{P}^-_b)$ and computes the
innovation and innovation covariance:
\begin{align}
  r^a &= z_{ab} - h_{ab}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^-_a, \hat{\mathbf{x}}^-_b),\\
  S_{ab} &= R^a + \tilde{H}_a \mathbf{P}^-_a \tilde{H}_a^\top
    + \tilde{H}_b \mathbf{P}^-_b \tilde{H}_b^\top \nonumber\\
    &\quad - \tilde{H}_a \Phi_a \Pi^a_{ab} \Phi_b^\top \tilde{H}_b^\top
    - (\cdot)^\top,
  \label{eq:imdcl_innov}
\end{align}
where $\tilde{H}_a = [-I_3\ 0_3]$ and $\tilde{H}_b = [I_3\ 0_3]$.
The gain factors for the measuring agent $a$ and the landmark $b$ are:
\begin{align}
  \Gamma_a &= \left( \Pi^a_{ab} \Phi_b^\top \tilde{H}_b^\top
    - \Phi_a^{-1} \mathbf{P}^-_a \tilde{H}_a^\top \right) S_{ab}^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:imdcl_gamma_a}\\
  \Gamma_b &= \left( \Phi_b^{-1} \mathbf{P}^-_b \tilde{H}_b^\top
    - \Pi^b_{ba} \Phi_a^\top \tilde{H}_a^\top \right) S_{ab}^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:imdcl_gamma_b}
\end{align}
Agent $a$ packs $\big(\Gamma_a, \Gamma_b,
\Phi_b^\top \tilde{H}_b^\top S_{ab}^{-1/2},
\Phi_a^\top \tilde{H}_a^\top S_{ab}^{-1/2}\big)$ into the update message
and broadcasts it to the whole reachable team.  Every other agent
$i \notin \{a,b\}$ derives its own gain locally from its $\Pi$ copies:
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_i = \Pi^i_{ib} \Phi_b^\top \tilde{H}_b^\top S_{ab}^{-1/2}
    - \Pi^i_{ia} \Phi_a^\top \tilde{H}_a^\top S_{ab}^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:imdcl_gamma_i}
\end{equation}
Finally every agent $i \in \mathcal{V}$ applies the correction:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\mathbf{x}}^+_i &= \hat{\mathbf{x}}^-_i + \Phi_i \Gamma_i
  \tilde{r}^a, \label{eq:imdcl_state}\\
  \mathbf{P}^+_i &= \mathbf{P}^-_i - \Phi_i \Gamma_i \Gamma_i^\top
  \Phi_i^\top, \label{eq:imdcl_cov}\\
  \Pi^i_{jl}(k{+}1) &= \Pi^i_{jl}(k) - \Gamma_j \Gamma_l^\top,
  \label{eq:imdcl_seed}
\end{align}
where $\tilde{r}^a = S_{ab}^{-1/2} r^a$.  The seed update
\eqref{eq:imdcl_seed} propagates the measurement benefit to all
cross-covariance pairs, and $\Phi_i$ is reset to $I$ after the update.

\subsection{Terrain and Simulation Environment}

Experiments run on 5 different real terrain patches from the TINItaly dataset (10\,m
resolution, Trentino Alps).
The \texttt{simulation.py} module runs a synchronous multi-agent loop
at 10\,Hz: at each tick every drone queries the terrain height,
computes its MPC command, updates IMDCL, reads the ARTVA signal, and
exchanges messages with in-range neighbors.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

Simulations run on real terrain scan data from the TINItaly dataset (10\,m
resolution, Trentino Alps), interpolated.  For each parameter combination, 5 distinct
terrain patches are extracted and 23 victim positions are sampled
uniformly at random per patch, with burial depth drawn from
$\mathcal{U}[1,5]$\,m.  A run is counted as a success when at least one PF-active drone
delivers a drift-corrected estimate whose $95\%$ confidence xy-ellipse
contains the true victim and has area $\le A_{\max} = \pi(5\,\text{m})^2$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:pf}), within the $600$\,s mission budget, runs exceeding this cutoff
are treated as operational failures, as a victim located too late offers
little survival benefit.

failures spread toward large, disjoint ellipses.  This confirms that
cooperative fusion~\eqref{eq:pf_fusion} drives the team toward a shared,
confident estimate rather than toward coincidentally close centroids.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a fully distributed multi-drone ARTVA search system
operating over real alpine terrain without any central coordinator,
combining Extremum Seeking, a cooperative 3-D Particle Filter, and
IMDCL for GPS-free localization, with success assessed through
drift-corrected confidence ellipses.

Over a $12420$-run parametric sweep the system delivers a median
planar localization error of $0.73$\,m and a median depth error of
$1.17$\,m, essentially invariant to the ARTVA noise level across
three decades.  Fleet size and workspace are the dominant factors:
3~drones suffice on a $100$\,m area ($95.6\%$), while 5 are needed
for comparable reliability on $200$\,m ($90.7\%$ vs.\ $73.9\%$ with
3); short communication radius is the main single-factor failure mode
($80.3\%$ at $r_c=25$\,m vs.\ $95.9\%$ at $120$\,m).

Future directions include: improving depth estimation accuracy (median
$1.17$\,m, with a favourable depth-dependent trend in
Fig.~\ref{fig:locerr}); extending the FSM to handle multiple
simultaneous victims; incorporating a realistic low-level attitude
controller; and profiling computational load on embedded
flight hardware.
